How the whole thing works

Five pictures: what the pieces are, how information moves, what the assistant does, how it fixes and improves itself, and how the dashboard grows new panels when you ask.

01

The architecture — four pieces

Your mail, calendar and notes stay where they are. A small program copies what is new into a brain — a folder of small, searchable text pages. Two things read that brain: Kiran, the assistant you chat with, and the dashboard in your browser. A watchdog checks that all of this is actually running, and texts you when it is not.

The one hard safety rule: the code that could send an email was never written. Kiran can prepare drafts in your Drafts folder, and only you can press send. No setting, no bug, and no clever instruction hidden in an email can change that.

02

Data flow — two loops

Information moves in two separate loops. One runs by itself every 15 minutes and keeps the brain current. The other runs when you ask a question.

04

Self-healing, and self-improvement

Self-healing is the small loop: notice a breakage, say so, and recover. It already works — here is a real morning, with no human involved:

Self-improvement is the big loop: the system collects its own failures and tries to fix its own code overnight. Three feeds fill one queue, and a wall of gates decides what is allowed to change without you.

The protected list — files the overnight fixer may never touch, no matter what: email drafting, phone alerts, secrets, the schedules, and the fixer itself. The rules live in a file only you can edit. A loop that could improve away its own limits isn't a tool, it's a gamble.

05

Adaptive panels — the dashboard grows on demand

Panels are data, not code. Each panel is a few rows in a small database, so making a new one takes a sentence, not a programmer. Click "+ panel" in the sidebar, or tell Kiran "make me a hiring tracker" — a new tab appears, born with everything the Prospects panel proved out.
